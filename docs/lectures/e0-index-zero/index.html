What a language model adds, and what it does notThe stack above the model exists to supply what the model lacks

What it adds

A reasoner that generalizes from text.

It reads an unfamiliar error, proposes a plan, writes the call, and explains itself. Classical agents never had this capability.

No persistent state

Every session starts empty. Context, memory, handoffs, and ledgers (E6–E7) exist to supply it.

No ground truth

It has no environment of its own. Tools, retrieval, and evaluators (E3–E5, E10–E11) exist to supply it.

No accountability

It cannot answer for anything. Governance (E0, E5, E9, E11) supplies it and cannot be automated.

The LLM agent loopThe classical definition with a model in the reasoner slot, and a halt condition

Perceive

prompt · tool results · files · events

Reason

one model call that emits a thought and a decision

Act

a tool call, or a final answer

Halt?

final answer · turn budget · cost budget · error

no → the observation goes back in as the next percept

ReAct (Yao et al., 2022): the canonical form

Thought → Action → Observation, until the model emits a final answer. Every later Evolution builds on this loop.

The halt condition is the part 2023 forgot

AutoGPT and BabyAGI (spring 2023): goals, no grounding, no budget, no evaluator. A loop with no halt runs until the budget is gone. Sortie 1 is 'a governed loop that halts.'

1 Foundation

What model, weights, data? Local or hosted?

✕ Cost and capability assumed, not measured

✕ Spirals; no halt; plans that never replan

What is in the window, compacted, persisted; who owns retention?

✕ Context exhaustion; stale facts recalled as truth

6 Coordination

Single, controller–worker, crew, graph, protocol (MCP/A2A)?

✕ Summary laundering; credit inferred, not earned

7 Evaluation & feedback

What checks it, what it optimizes, how failure is detected?

✕ Gaming the gate; judge self-preference

8 Governance & human

Where is the human: index zero, in/on the loop, absent? What is attested?

✕ Governance theater; rubber-stamp attestation

Scheduled, observed, rolled back, billed: how?

✕ No rollback; unmeasured cost and latency

The Zero Doctrine and the covenantThe human is index zero, and the obligation runs both ways

[0]

First in priority.

Your intent drives every decision.

Final in authority.

You sign off, not the model.

Cannot be automated away.

Accountability is a property of a person, not a pipeline.

How 'zero' evolved: zero overhead → zero drift → index zero.

The covenant: five clauses, two parties bound

1

The human defines intent (PRD, ADRs, acceptance criteria).

2

The agent operates within constraints and flags violations.

3

Verification is mutual: tests, checks, evidence.

4

The human attests completion after observing artifacts.

5

Artifacts survive sessions.

Two sides: gates constrain agents, and humans must engage. Gates alone produce theater. “Specification without governance drifts; governance without methodology is theater; methodology without specification is arbitrary.”

slide 01

The Human Is Index Zero

This lecture carries content rather than logistics, and the orientation deck holds the mechanics of the semester. The lecture leaves you with four maps and one posture. The four maps are the seven-layer stack, the five-stage progression to agentic AI, the definition of an agent from PEAS to the nine components, and the framework board with every box assigned to an Evolution. The posture is that the human is index zero, and the Degradation Pattern describes what happens when the human stops being index zero. Four words describe the semester (survey, build, govern, defend), and this Evolution covers the survey and govern pair.

slide 02

The question behind the course

The course asks two questions. The survey question asks why: what problem each design answered and what the design cost, rather than what is fashionable this month. The build question asks whether you can make such a system and answer for it. Tree Hollow is your first reading because it states the stake: a system can produce an answer without being able to answer for it. Ask the class who answered for the last piece of generated code they shipped, and wait for answers. The definition that runs the semester follows from that question. A language model is a reasoner with no persistent state, no ground truth, and no accountability. Every component this course studies is an attempt to supply one of those three things from outside the model. Governance supplies the third, and no software can automate it, because accountability belongs to a person. [~7 min]

slide 03

Map one: the Layers of AI

The first map has seven layers, and you can read it top down or bottom up. Agentic AI sits on top with memory, planning, tool use, and autonomous execution. Under it sit generative AI, deep learning, neural networks, machine learning, and classical AI, and at the bottom sits the data foundation: ownership, definitions, quality, lineage. The figure draws the layers as a widening stack because every agentic system stands on all of them. Nothing at the top replaces anything below. Classical planning did not die; it returned as 'planning' in LLM agents. For this reason a Design Analysis starts at the bottom, with the question of what the system stands on. [~5 min]

slide 04

…and the one that is easy to forget

The data foundation is easy to forget because it is unglamorous and because its cost lands later, on someone else. The framework image lists four words for it: ownership, definitions, quality, lineage. Each one returns as a failure later in the semester. Ownership asks on whose behalf the agent may act. Definitions matter because the agent inherits every ambiguity in your schema, and 'done' is the most dangerous word in the course. Quality matters because retrieval returns what is stored: in Evolution 7 you will poison your own memory store with one false fact and watch it propagate. Lineage matters because without provenance a polished summary becomes a fact, which is the ten-deep summary-laundering chain you read in Evolution 9. The Sprint and Drift essay 'Newtonian motion' argues that the cost of generated code is deferred, never erased. [~2 min]

slide 05

Map two: from AI & ML to Agentic AI

The second map runs five stages left to right, and the unit of work changes at each stage. AI and ML turns data into a decision. Deep learning stacks networks for harder decisions. Generative AI produces an artifact: text, code, an image. AI agents take on a task: they call tools, plan, remember, and keep a human in the loop. Agentic AI automates a process: many agents, protocols between them, delegation, intent preserved across handoffs, rollback, evaluators, cost management, observability, governance. Evolutions 1 through 7 build the AI Agents column on your system, and Evolutions 8 through 12 build the Agentic AI column. [~4 min]

slide 06

The road map

The road map shows the same five stages as stops. You consume stops one through three rather than build them: the models, the networks, and the generative capability come from a vendor or an open-weight release. You build stops four and five in this course: an agent that takes on a task, then an agentic system that automates a process. Keep this picture, because each Evolution states which stop it is at. [~1 min]

slide 07

The framework board, read as rings

The framework board can also be read as rings out from the foundation: foundation, creation, core systems, action, full automation. Rings state how far a system's capability extends outward. The columns on the previous slide state when each capability arrived in the field's history. Hold both readings. A system that claims ring five and runs on ring two is a generative model with a loop bolted on, with no memory, no evaluator, and no governance, and vendors sell such systems as agentic. By the end of this lecture you will be able to say why that gap matters. The exercise for Evolution 0 is to mark every box on the board for which you can cite a concrete system, and the blanks become your reading list for the semester. [~3 min]

slide 08

Every box on the board is assigned to a week

This table gives the fill-in order. I have placed every box on the framework board in one of the twelve Evolutions. Two features of the table need attention. First, the middle is inverted from the catalog order: tools and MCP come before context and memory. Since 2024, tool results have filled most of an agent harness's context window, so the course teaches tools before context management, and MCP is a tool protocol that requires function calling understood first. Second, the two starred Evolutions run on a local open-weight model no matter which route you chose, because tool calling with a small model is brittle, and the brittleness shows you the mechanism, and because embeddings are cheap locally. You do not need to memorize the table. You need to know it exists so that in every Evolution you can say where you are on the board. [~3 min]

slide 09

What a language model adds, and what it does not

Before the definition of an agent, the reasoner in the middle of it needs a precise statement. A language model adds a reasoner that generalizes from text. Classical agents never had that reasoner, and its arrival is why the agent loops of 2023 appeared. The model lacks three things: persistent state, ground truth, and accountability. Those three things organize the semester. Context and memory supply state. Tools, retrieval, and evaluators supply ground truth. Governance supplies accountability, and more software cannot supply it, because accountability is a property of a person. [~2 min]

slide 10

What is an agent? The definition that predates the LLM

The definition of an agent predates the language model and still holds. Russell and Norvig define an agent as anything that perceives its environment through sensors and acts on it through actuators, and the agent function maps percept sequences to actions. You specify an agent by PEAS: performance measure, environment, actuators, sensors. The performance measure deserves attention, because the 2023 loops never wrote one down, and for this reason they ran until the budget was gone. The five agent types form a ladder: reflex, model-based, goal-based, utility-based, learning. BDI and FIPA supply the multi-agent vocabulary, so when you meet CrewAI in Evolution 8 you will recognize a 1990s design with a language model in the reasoner slot. Your first exercise in Evolution 0 is to specify your intended system by PEAS. [~3 min]

slide 11

The LLM agent loop

The LLM agent loop puts a language model in the reasoner slot. The loop perceives the prompt, tool results, files, and events. It reasons with one model call that emits a thought and a decision. It acts with either a tool call or a final answer. Then it asks the question the whole loop turns on: halt? If the answer is no, the observation goes back in as the next percept. ReAct, from Yao and colleagues in 2022, is the canonical form: thought, action, observation, interleaved. Every later Evolution adds tools, memory, coordination, and evaluation to this loop. ReAct lacked memory, a budget, and verification. The halt condition is what 2023 forgot: AutoGPT and BabyAGI were this loop with a goal and nothing to stop it. Sortie 1 is a loop that halts, on every input class, within budget, with every turn in the ledger. [~3 min]

slide 12

The nine components of a Design Analysis

The nine components are how you take every system apart this semester, and their order matters. The list runs from what the system stands on, to what it sees, to how it thinks, to how it acts, to what it remembers, to how it coordinates, to what checks it, to where the human is, to how it runs. Each component carries a question and, in teal, the failure that appears when the component is missing or weak. The failures preview the incident casebook: injection is a perception failure, a spiral is a planning failure, summary laundering is a coordination failure, and governance theater is a failure of component eight. A Design Analysis consists of nine short answers and one paragraph on why. [~3 min]

slide 13

A worked Design Analysis: chat assistant vs. agent harness

The Evolution 0 exercise, done on a slide, compares a chat assistant (left column) with an agent harness such as Claude Code or Codex CLI (right column) across the nine rows. The foundation is the same hosted model. Perception changes: the harness sees the repo, the shell, tool results, and an instruction file. Planning changes: the chat assistant has one turn, and the human is the loop, while the harness runs an open ReAct loop with plan mode and subagents. Tools: the chat assistant has none, and the harness has a built-in set plus MCP with permission prompts and hooks. Memory: both are bound to the window, but the harness compacts and reads an instruction file. Coordination: a single session against a main session plus subagents. Evaluation: in both, the human does most of it. Governance: the chat assistant has a human on every turn by construction, and the harness has a human in the loop per tool call, with attestation implicit. Runtime: a web page against a terminal with background and cloud sessions. The why paragraph follows from the rows. The harness owns the loop so that the vendor can ship tools, hooks, compaction, and subagents as features, and that ownership of the loop is what makes it an agent. The cost is that governance is per call and implicit, and nothing records what was attested. gzkit, the reference design, fills that gap one tier up. The comparison does not rank the chat assistant below the harness: the chat assistant is a workflow with the human as the loop, and a workflow is the right design wherever the path is known in advance. [~4 min]

slide 14

Two distinctions to carry out of this Evolution

Two distinctions carry out of this Evolution. The first comes from Anthropic's 'Building Effective Agents': a workflow calls the model along code paths you wrote, and an agent lets the model direct its own control flow. Anthropic recommends the workflow wherever the path is known in advance, and the open loop only where it is not, and in Evolution 2 you will write down which one you chose and why. The second distinction is historical. The field carried PEAS, planning, and coordination protocols forward from the pre-LLM era. The loops of 2023 dropped explicit state, halt conditions, and evaluation against an environment, and the field has rediscovered each of them since. Reinforcement learning had already described reward hacking, so gaming the gate has a history older than the language model. [~2 min]

slide 15

Why governance: the Degradation Pattern

The Degradation Pattern, from gzkit's why.md, is the reason I built the course the way I did. In week one you prompt carefully and read every line. In week two you skim the diff and trust the green checkmarks. In week three you batch approvals, and 'looks good' becomes reflexive. In week four you approve pull requests you did not read for code you do not understand. Nobody decides to lose control; control slips away one 'looks good' at a time. The failure has three names, and they describe the same event: cognitive surrender, in which you delegate the judgment rather than the task; mediated observation, in which you see the summary and never the artifact; and governance theater, in which every check passes and nobody verified anything. Ask the class where on this curve each of them was the previous Evolution. The first exercise in chapter three is a one-week log of your own review behavior. [~4 min]

slide 16

The Zero Doctrine and the covenant

The Zero Doctrine answers the Degradation Pattern: the human is index zero. First in priority, because your intent drives every decision. Final in authority, because you sign off, and the model does not. And the position cannot be automated away, because accountability is a property of a person. The word zero has its own history in the reference design: it began as zero overhead, became zero drift, and settled as index zero. The covenant has five clauses, and both parties are bound. The human defines intent. The agent operates within constraints and flags violations. Verification is mutual. The human attests after observing the artifacts rather than the summary. Artifacts survive sessions. The covenant has two sides: gates constrain the agent, and the human must engage. Gates alone produce theater. The three concerns (specification, methodology, governance) need each other, and the sentence on the slide is the founding argument of the reference design. It comes from a conversation in January in which the model said only specification matters and the operator refused. [~3 min]

slide 17

Five gates, two lanes, and the command doctrine

Five gates enforce the covenant. ADR records intent before implementation. TDD derives tests from the spec, with the red witnessed before the green. The Docs gate checks that what is written matches what runs. BDD supplies acceptance scenarios for anything with an external contract. Human means that someone looked at the artifacts and signed. The gates run in two lanes. Lite is gates one and two, and the weekly Evolutions live there. Heavy is all five, and the Final System lives there. There are two lanes rather than one because ceremony must earn its place. Human attestation at the brief level is universal in both lanes, and there is no self-close path. The command doctrine is the source of the flight vocabulary in this course. Degani and Wiener's Four P's run philosophy, policies, procedures, practices. The cockpit is the model because it is the one place where engineers have built authority over an automated system for decades. The doctrine has four rules: accountability is non-transferable; authority must be instrumented, not asserted; the model is a crew resource, not a crew member; evidence is artifacts, not narration. [~3 min]

slide 18

Where a covenant sits among the governance frameworks

The posture needs its place among the governance frameworks, so that nobody mistakes it for the whole of AI governance. Policy frameworks (NIST's AI Risk Management Framework, the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001) tell an organization what must be true, and they are silent on what one engineer and one agent do in one session. Frontier-lab safety frameworks and system cards govern the model, and in Evolution 11 you will read one as a Design Analysis. The practitioner literature on auditing describes an accountability gap: audits without access, standards without enforcement, attestations nobody observed. A covenant is one engineer's answer to that gap. It sits below policy, inside the session, at the level of the artifact. It cannot make a model safe, certify an organization, or substitute for law. Its scope is one build, and it makes the engineer who signed answerable for that build. [~2 min]

slide 19

The instrument: the judgment loop

The judgment loop from CIDM 6330 is the survey thread's instrument, and it closes every Design Analysis. State the decision in one sentence. State the characteristic the decision privileged. State the cost. State the failure mode, which is what breaks first when the assumption behind the decision stops holding. State the preservation mechanism, which is what keeps the intent alive after the person who decided has left the session. The format on the right is short on purpose: about a hundred and fifty words, nine lines, one paragraph, five items. [~2 min]
