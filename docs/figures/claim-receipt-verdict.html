Interaction 2 · Reference design · 2026-05-24 · E9 slide 20

The ten-deep chain: how a summary becomes a fact

In May 2026 the reference design ran a chain of subagents ten deep, each summarizing the layer below it. Each layer's polished summary became the next layer's fact, and by the end nobody in the chain had looked at the primary evidence. No single agent lied. Step up the chain one hop at a time, then run the same chain with receipts required. The deck does not quote the chain's text, so the summaries here are illustrative, built on the deck's worked receipt (3 tests run, 1 failure).

MAST reading from the deck: FC2, because information passed without checking, and FC3, because the check verified the summary and never the work. In Appendix C terms it is #3 fabrication, without any single agent lying. The deck asks the instructor to verify the date, the depth, and the rule's wording against AGENTS.md before class.

E9 slide 6 · chapter 18

Adversarial validation in three tiers

Independence is the property you are buying. Size is not.

Tier 1 · cross-vendor

A different model family. It does not share the worker's training data, tokenizer, or house style, so it is least likely to find the worker's mistake plausible. Model evaluators recognize and score their own generations higher than equal work from others (Panickssery, Bowman, & Feng, 2024).

Tier 2 · independent subagent

Same family, fresh context, no access to the worker's transcript. Cheaper to arrange. It shares the family's blind spots.

Tier 3 · human

The Chase, or you. Slowest. The only tier that can attest.

On OpenCode, tier 1 is cheap this term. If your worker runs on Big Pickle, run the adversary on an OpenCode Go model from a named family: GLM, DeepSeek, Kimi, Qwen, MiniMax, or MiMo. Big Pickle is a stealth model whose family is not published, so write “worker family: undisclosed” and run two adversaries from two Go families. With one family only, write “degraded mode” on the card.

The adversary gets the claim and the receipt. It never gets the worker's summary, its reasoning, or the answer you planted. The deck's worked agent file denies the adversary edit, bash, and webfetch, and defines NOT-REFUTED inside the prompt, because a model left to its own words will write “verified.”

E9 slide 8 · try it

Locks: who may work on what

  1. Acquire atomically. Create locks/<resource>.lock with os.open(path, O_CREAT | O_EXCL). The operating system lets exactly one caller create it. Ledger: lock_acquired.
  2. Deny the second. A second worker's create fails with FileExistsError. It returns BLOCKED with the holder's name. Ledger: lock_denied.
  3. Release with the token. Only the caller holding the token may delete the lock. Ledger: lock_released.
  4. Expire, then reclaim. If now > acquired_at + ttl_s, the holder is presumed dead. The next worker records the expiry first. Ledger: lock_expired, then lock_acquired.

Ledger, the lock's witness

The resource name, the 120-second TTL, and the clock are illustrative. The falsifier is a lock that changed hands with no ledger event, because then nobody can tell whether a collision happened.

E9 slide 17 · counterpoint

“Don't Build Multi-Agents”

Walden Yan, Cognition, June 2025. Two principles from the team that builds a coding agent.

“Share context, and share full agent traces, not just individual messages.”Yan (2025), principle 1
“Actions carry implicit decisions, and conflicting decisions carry bad results.”Yan (2025), principle 2

Mechanism. Two subagents given parts of one task make decisions the other cannot see. Yan's example: one builds a background in the wrong visual style, the other builds a bird that matches neither, and the lead cannot reconcile them.

Design choice. One single-threaded agent with continuous context, which compresses its history with a model when the window runs out.

Read against Anthropic's post. The two do not conflict. Research is breadth-first and splits cleanly; coding carries decisions forward. Sortie 9 does not ask you to parallelize anything. It asks you to check one claim with an agent that shares nothing with the worker.

What coordination cost Anthropic's research system

A Claude Opus 4 lead plans and starts Claude Sonnet 4 subagents that search in parallel, and a CitationAgent ties the report's claims to sources at the end (June 2025).

15×tokens used relative to a chat (single agents: about 4×)
80%of performance variance on BrowseComp explained by token usage alone
90.2%better than single-agent Claude Opus 4 on Anthropic's internal research eval, which is not a public benchmark

Early subagents duplicated searches and spawned too many tasks for simple queries; the fix was detailed task descriptions and explicit effort rules. Subagents write output to a filesystem, which turns a relayed claim into an artifact the next agent can check. The authors say multi-agent fits breadth-first research and fits badly where all agents must share one context, including most coding.

What the figure leaves out

  • The other two dated gzkit episodes. In June 2026 Gate-5 attestations were attributed to an operator who never gave them, and the reference design answered with gz obpi repudiate, which appends a repudiation and never erases the attestation. On 2026-08-21 a stale marker file licensed about 350 lines with no dispatch and no review.
  • Delegation across organizations. A2A v1.0 can sign an Agent Card, which proves who sent an artifact and says nothing about whether it is true. An A2A artifact enters your ledger as a claim with a verified sender.
  • Marketplaces. AP2 mandates prove what a human authorized for a payment. No protocol in the deck carries a receipt for “tests pass” across an agent boundary, so that job stays in your harness.
  • The ReviewResult from Sortie 8 and the two-stage review. The figure draws one worker and one adversary.
