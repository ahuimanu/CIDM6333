How the cost column was built

The paper does not measure FLOPs. It multiplies training time by the number of GPUs and by a rated speed per GPU, and it prints the rated speeds in footnote 5: 2.8, 3.7, 6.0, and 9.5 TFLOPS for the K80, K40, M40, and P100. Both Transformers trained on one machine with 8 P100s.

41.0 or 41.8? The §6.1 text says the big English-French model “achieves a BLEU score of 41.0”. Table 2 prints 41.8. The paper does not say which is right. This page plots the table's 41.8 and marks 41.0 with a dashed ring, so the gap stays visible.

The paper prints one cost per Transformer, spanning both language pairs. It states the method once and does not list the time and GPU inputs behind the earlier models' costs.

The paper sets no threshold and reports one run per row, so it gives no way to tell whether a 0.1 BLEU gap would survive a second run. The slider is a pre-registration you choose before you look. Perplexities are per wordpiece, and the paper warns they “should not be compared to per-word perplexities.” In row (D), ε_ls = 0.0 has better perplexity than base and worse BLEU. Switch the metric to watch it cross the line. §5.4 predicts this: label smoothing “hurts perplexity, as the model learns to be more unsure, but improves accuracy and BLEU score.”

The learning-rate schedule

lrate = d_model−0.5 · min(step_num−0.5, step_num · warmup_steps−1.5)
4,000
d_model
your setting paper: warmup 4,000, d_model 512

Below the peak, the second term is smaller, so the rate climbs in a straight line. At step = warmup_steps the two terms are equal. After it, the first term is smaller, so the rate falls as 1/√step. A larger d_model lowers the whole curve. The y-axis is fixed so the curves can be compared. The base model trained for 100,000 steps; the big model for 300,000. The paper pairs this schedule with Adam, β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.98, ε = 10⁻⁹.

What the attention pictures show

This is a schematic redraw. The paper does not publish the attention weights, so the lines show only the links the captions name. Line width carries no value. Both figures come from encoder self-attention in layer 5 of 6. The paper's Figure 3 also shows fainter links from making, and Figure 4 also shows every link for head 5; neither is drawn here.

Attention weights are not explanations. Jain and Wallace (2019) tested recurrent models with attention on text classification and question answering. They found that attention weights often disagreed with other measures of which inputs mattered, and that quite different attention patterns could give the same prediction. A picture of one head on one sentence is a hypothesis about the model. The paper's own captions hedge with apparently and seems.
Table 4, redrawn · §6.3

A second task: English constituency parsing

The authors trained a 4-layer Transformer with d_model = 1024 on the Wall Street Journal portion of the Penn Treebank, about 40,000 training sentences, and in a semi-supervised setting with about 17 million sentences. They tuned only dropout, learning rates, and beam size, on the Section 22 development set. Scores are F1 on Section 23.

Bars start at 0, so their lengths are honest and the gaps look small.
earlier parser Transformer (4 layers)

The text says the model gives “better results than all previously reported models with the exception of the Recurrent Neural Network Grammar,” which is Dyer et al. (2016) at 93.3. Table 4 also lists Luong et al. (2015), a multi-task model, at 93.0, above the Transformer's 92.7. Read the claim against the table, as you would read a model's summary against its trace.

Table 3 as a model of evaluation design

Table 3 is the closest thing in the paper to the delta gate you build in Sortie 10. It holds one base fixed, changes one thing per row, and keeps the set it tunes on apart from the set it reports. It also has gaps that your gate must close.

The big row changes five settings at once, so it cannot say which change helped.Split a multi-change commit before the gate, or the delta cannot name a cause.

The E10 deck's test for pre-registration applies to Table 3 as well: “could the evidence have come out against you, and did you say in advance what that would look like?” Table 3 passes the first half; the paper is silent on the second.

What the figure leaves out

  • Figure 5 of the paper, and the full head-5 panel of Figure 4. The architecture and Table 1 are in Figure 01b, The 2017 Transformer.
  • Table 2's Transformer scores come from averaged checkpoints (the last 5 for base, the last 20 for big). Table 3 uses no averaging and a different test set, so its base 25.8 does not compare with Table 2's 27.3.
  • The big English-French model used P_drop = 0.1 instead of 0.3 (§6.1).
  • BLEU scores from different papers can depend on tokenization and the scoring script (Post, 2018). Table 2 compares numbers as each paper reported them.
  • The paper reports no variance, seeds, or repeated runs for any table.
