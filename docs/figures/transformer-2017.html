The paper scales by 1/√d_k because “for large values of d_k, the dot products grow large in magnitude, pushing the softmax function into regions where it has extremely small gradients” (§3.2.1). The grid on the right lets you watch that happen.

64

Rows are queries and columns are keys; each row sums to 1. The vectors are random, not trained, so the pattern means nothing about these words. The grid shows the arithmetic only. Turn scaling off and raise d_k: each row collapses onto one key, which is the saturation the paper describes. Masked cells are the “−∞” entries of §3.2.3.

§3.5 · Positional encoding

Word order, written in waves

PE(pos, 2i) = sin(pos / 10000^(2i/d_model))
PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos(pos / 10000^(2i/d_model))
dimension 0dimension 127 of 512 →
−10+1rows: positions 0–99, top to bottom

Each pair of dimensions is a sine and a cosine at its own wavelength, from 2π up to 10000·2π. Low dimensions change every position; high dimensions barely change across a sentence. The sum of this pattern and the token embedding is what enters the first layer. Many newer models encode position differently, for example by rotating queries and keys, but the problem is the same one: attention alone ignores order.

Self-attention is cheaper than recurrence “when the sequence length n is smaller than the representation dimensionality d, which is most often the case” (§4). In 2017 n was a sentence. In 2026 n is a context window, far to the right of the crossing. Its O(1) sequential operations are why it trains in parallel on GPUs. Order of growth only; constants and kernel optimizations are ignored.

What this redraw adds or changes

  • Layout, component names, and color roles follow Figure 1 and Figure 2 of the paper. Colors are re-tinted so the figure reads in light and dark themes.
  • The dashed return arrow on the right is not in the paper. It draws the sentence in §3 that says the model is auto-regressive.
  • The decoder-only view is not in the paper. It shows the design that GPT (Radford et al., 2018) and later chat models adopted.
  • The 2017 model normalizes after each residual addition (post-norm). Most later models normalize before the sub-layer (pre-norm), which trains more stably at depth.
  • For the same model placed inside the agent loop, see Figure 01, Two Loops.
